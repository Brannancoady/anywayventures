A pattern I see in almost every business I walk into: a company-wide AI licence rolled out eighteen months ago, a handful of enthusiasts using it heavily, most people using it to draft emails, and the core processes — quoting, onboarding, reconciliation, reporting — exactly as they were.

Licences don't change workflows. Workflows change when someone sits with the person doing the work, maps where the hours go, builds the thing that removes them, and stays until the new way is the habit. That is not a training problem and it is not a strategy problem; it is a build-and-embed problem.

The practical answer is a short embedded sprint: two to three weeks inside the business, the top one or two processes automated and running before the end, the team watching it happen so they own it after. It costs a fraction of the transformation programme and it produces something you can measure in the following month's numbers.

The measure of success is boring on purpose: what is still running, unattended, six months after the outside help has left.
